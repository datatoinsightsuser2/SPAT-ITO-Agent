## General Questions
1. Can I travel with my pet in the cabin?

Some airlines allow small pets in the cabin that can easily fit under your seat - typically less than 20 pounds in weight and 9 inches in height from floor to the top of the head. Check with the airline for exact policies and book with them directly in advance. Larger pets usually travel in the cargo hold and Smart Pet Air Travel can help you with that.

2. Is it safe to fly pets in cargo on an aircraft?

Yes, it is safe to fly pets internationally. In the U.S., 2 million animals flew on commercial air carriers in 2017 (DOT report) with a very low incident rate of approximately 0.0048% (24 incidents total). Cargo holds are insulated, temperature-controlled, and equipped with ventilation systems like passenger compartments. Major pet-friendly airlines prioritize pet well-being, ensuring safe boarding and disembarking procedures. If you want to learn how live animals are carried in cargo on a plane, this YouTube video from an airline captain does a great job of explaining: https://youtu.be/79tZMJU-HB4

3. What documents are required for international pet travel?

Requirements vary by country. Generally, you’ll need health certificates, vaccination records, and import permits. Most countries require that your pet has a 15-digit ISO microchip implanted. For cats and dogs, rabies vaccine is required to be given concurrently or after microchipping and is in the system for minimum 21 days before travel. Consult with a professional pet shipper for your specific case.

4. Who issues international health certificates for pets in US– can my vet do it?

USDA is the government agency that issues health certificates for pet travel outside the country. It is a 2- step process. In step 1, a USDA accredited vet examines your pet and reviews vaccination records, typically 10 days before travel. USDA accredited vets then submit the health certificate to USDA for further stamping/endorsement. Ask your vet if they are USDA accredited and offer an international travel health certificate through VEHCS system. If not, you can find one near you by using this tool:https://vsapps.aphis.usda.gov/vsps/public/VetSearch.do

5. What type of crate is required for pets to travel in cargo?

These crates should be appropriately sized, comfortable, and secure. They must allow pets to stand, turn, and lie down comfortably. The crate needs to be ventilated on 4 sides with solid top and leakproof floor. The crate needs to be sturdy (hard plastic or wood or metal), with metal nuts and bolts and a metal gate. The door should have a reliable locking system, and the crate itself must be non-collapsible.
